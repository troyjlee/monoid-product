# Paper correspondence

Part of the [MonoidProduct guide](README.md).

This record compares the Lean statements of the MonoidProduct library with
the results of *The quantum query complexity of the semigroup product
problem* (Troy Lee and Miklos Santha). For each paper result it lists the
corresponding Lean declaration, its source file, and any difference in
hypotheses, constants or scope.

Paper results are cited by their numbers in the version of the paper
accompanying this release, with the LaTeX label from the paper's source in
parentheses in the first column (the labels are stable across revisions;
the numbers may not be). "(text)" marks a claim made in the running text.
Declarations are fully qualified. File paths are
relative to the repository root, except that a path beginning with
`QuantumQueryComplexity/` refers to the
[QuantumQueryComplexity](https://github.com/troyjlee/quantum-query-complexity)
dependency, which provides the general adversary, duality and composition
machinery.

Lean checks the encoded statements and proofs; comparing those statements
and definitions with the paper also requires mathematical interpretation.

## Conventions

- **Problem.** `Prod_{M,G,n}` is `fun x : Fin n → σ => wordProd letter x`
  (written `∏ i, m (x i)` in the commutative files) for a letter map
  `letter : σ → M` from a finite type `σ`; the paper's alphabet `G` is the
  image of `letter`, and the full alphabet is `letter = id`. Products of
  nonempty words in a semigroup are `semigroupProd n : (Fin (n + 1) → S) → S`.
- **Product breadth.** `MonoidProduct.IsBreadthBound letter b` says that
  every word has a product core of at most `b` positions, and
  `MonoidProduct.breadth letter` is the least such `b` (the paper's
  `β_G(M)`, Eq. (1)). It is `0` when no bound exists, so theorems
  about monoids that may have infinite breadth take a bound `b` as a
  hypothesis.
- **Strong certificates.** For `f : Finset U → O`, the paper's `b_f(U)`
  (Eq. (33)) is `MonoidProduct.Certificates.strongCertificateSize f` and the
  extension monoid `M_f` is `MonoidProduct.Certificates.ExtensionMonoid f`.
  A record `x_i ∈ U ∪ {⊥}` is an `Option U`, with `none` the null record
  `⊥`.
- **Graphs.** A graph on the known vertex set `V` is its edge set, a
  `Finset (MonoidProduct.Graph.Edge V)`. `Edge V` is the set of unordered
  pairs of distinct vertices (`Sym2 V` without its diagonal), and
  `MonoidProduct.Graph.toGraph` turns an edge set into a Mathlib
  `SimpleGraph V` on all of `V`, so isolated vertices are kept. The paper's
  `b_f` for graphs is `strongCertificateSize f` over this universe. The
  graph query models are `qList f n` (edge lists `Fin n → Option (Edge V)`),
  `qMatrix f` (one bit per pair, `Edge V → Bool`) and `qAdj f deg` (valid
  adjacency arrays with the public degree vector `deg`); `qAdjEdges f m` is
  the worst case of `qAdj` over the degree vectors of graphs with `m`
  edges, the paper's `Q_adj(f; m)`.
- **Adversary bound and duals.** `ADV±` is `QuantumQueryComplexity.advPM`
  (`advPMOn` on a promise). An all-pairs dual of cost `T`
  (Eq. (3)) is `HasDual f T` (`HasDualOn` on a promise).
- **Query models.** `qQuery f ε` is the bounded-error query complexity with
  the library's native oracle, which swaps a blank answer register with the
  queried letter. `oneHotQQuery f ε` uses the whole-element XOR value oracle
  of the paper. The two agree up to a factor two in each direction; results
  whose constant depends on the model are stated in both.
- **Constants.** Each `O(·)`, `Ω(·)` and "absolute constant" of the paper is
  an explicit numeral in Lean. Recurring ones: a cost-`T` dual gives
  `Q_{1/3} ≤ 8192·(1 + T)` (`uniformExtractionConstant`), whose absorbed
  form for `√(n·min{n,b})` bounds is `2^18·√(n·min{n,b})`; and
  `(7/1376)·ADV± ≤ Q_{1/3}`.
- **Logarithms.** `Real.log` is natural, `Real.logb 2` is binary and
  `Nat.clog 2` is the ceiling of `log₂`. The paper's `L(n) = 2 + log₂(n+2)`
  is `MonoidProduct.paperLog n`, and `L_n = ⌈log₂(n+1)⌉` in the ordered
  results.

## 6. Applications: matroid bases and graph certificates

Section 6 (`sec:commutative-applications`) is formalized. Sections 6.1
(`sec:strong-certificates`), 6.2 (`sec:matroid-bases`) and 6.3
(`sec:graph-certificates`) are formalized: the representation of graphs,
the three query models and their comparison, the strong-certificate sizes
and query bounds for the component partition, two-colourings and cycle
detection, Proposition 34, planarity, every fixed proper minor-closed class
(through graph minors and Mader's theorem), outerplanarity, embeddability in
a fixed surface and bounded treewidth.

Some of these notions are defined combinatorially, with their equivalence to
the topological notions cited, not formalized:
- planarity, by a rotation system (a cyclic order of the edges at each
  vertex) whose faces satisfy Euler's formula in every component with an
  edge, a combinatorial embedding of genus `0`;
- outerplanarity, by planarity of the graph with a new vertex adjacent to
  every vertex (Chartrand–Harary);
- embeddability in a surface, by embedding schemes (rotation systems with
  twisted edges) and the Euler genus of their traced faces.

Graph minors (by minor models) and tree decompositions are the usual
combinatorial definitions.

Some statements are generic infrastructure whose hypotheses assume a
certificate or edge bound: the `_of_le` and `_of_bound` query forms,
`sparseYes_strongCertificateSize_le` and
`strongCertificateSize_le_mul_of_linear`. Every application also has an
unconditional endpoint about its actual property or output, listed in its
own row.

Section 6.4 (`sec:failure-profiles`) is formalized: both failure profiles, their certificates and
query bounds, the bridge, articulation-point and connectivity readouts, the
enumeration lower bound Eq. (41), and Proposition 35 with both halves for
both profiles. The paper cites the sparse vertex-connectivity certificate
(Nagamochi–Ibaraki); here it is proved, with at most `2kv` edges, by a
greedy construction and a Mader-type count rather than by
Nagamochi–Ibaraki's scan-first search.

## Not formalized in this release

All numbered results of Section 6 are formalized. Some notions are defined
combinatorially, with their equivalence to the topological notions cited:
planarity (rotation systems of genus `0`), outerplanarity (planarity of the
apex graph) and embeddability in a surface (embedding schemes and the Euler
genus); see Section 6 above.

Every other numbered theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary of the paper
has a Lean counterpart above. The following parts of examples and remarks
are cited from the literature and are not formalized:

- Example 1: the maximum-finding lower bound;
- Example 2: the query lower bound `Ω(min{n,√(nm)})`;
- the remark after Proposition 35 (`prop:failure-profile-complexity`)
  citing the minimum-cut algorithm of Apers and Lee, which decides
  `k`-edge-connectivity with `Õ(v^{3/2})` matrix queries;
- the remarks of Section 6.3 citing Childs and Kothari: their `O(v^{3/2})`
  matrix bound for sparse graph properties, and minor-closed properties
  with `o(v^{3/2})` matrix queries;
- the paragraph "Relation to earlier methods" of Section 6.2, comparing
  with Karger's sampling bound and the decision-tree theorem of Beigi and
  Taghavi, and the comparisons with Dürr et al. and DeLorenzo–Kimmel–Witter
  in Section 6.3; these are comparisons with prior work, and the Lean
  bounds are stated on their own.

Some statements are formalized in a slightly different form:

- Lemma 38, Lemma 45: every clause is proved for
  general parameters; the packaged cost bounds are proved in the iterated
  form used by Theorem 46 (see Remark 47).
- Several intermediate lemmas of Section 4 and Section 10 are stated for
  adversary duals (`HasDual`); the query forms follow from the library's
  conversions.
